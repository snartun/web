# New casual game types for a browser game hub (single-player and 1v1 online, phone browser, global and Türkiye, 2024–2026)

Scope note: about 20 tool calls. Hard per-game play counts for web portals are not published. Poki and CrazyGames publish platform totals and category pages ordered by popularity or votes, not per-game numbers. Store-side numbers come from AppMagic (via PocketGamer.biz), Sensor Tower's auto-generated quarterly country blog pages, and third-party estimators (AppGoblin, Udonis, ActivePlayer). The search tool itself flagged the Sensor Tower blog pages as possibly AI-generated, so read them as indicative only. Everything marked "Inference" is my reasoning, not a sourced fact.

## Q1: Which multiplayer, 2-player and single-player casual types show demand (web portals, app stores, Türkiye)?

### Takeaway
Demand evidence points to a few clusters:
- Physics sports duels (basketball, pool, soccer, archery, sniper ragdoll) dominate the 2-player shelves on web portals.
- Party and arena multiplayer (kart battles, tag, hazard/party games, io arenas) top Poki's multiplayer votes.
- Io-style "grow and eat" games are still huge in stores: Hole.io had 119.1M downloads in 2025, a record year.
- In Türkiye, a "2 3 4 Player Mini Games" bundle app sits in the Android top-3 casual downloads, and 101 Okey is among the top Android grossers.
- Block puzzle and tile/mahjong lead global single-player downloads, but the hub already has block puzzle.

### Inferences
- A "2 3 4 Player Mini Games"-type bundle sits in the TR top-3 casual downloads, and the 2-player shelves on portals are dominated by short physics sports duels. Together this suggests the strongest demand signal for this hub is a pack of quick 1v1 physics duels: basketball shots, soccer/penalty, archery or sniper ragdoll, sumo/bumper push, air hockey. Pool and table games (Tavla/Okey) are a second cluster, with very high demand in TR.
- Okey/101 is a 4-player tile game with heavy rules and established apps that have voice chat. Tavla (backgammon) is 1v1 and turn-based, a much better fit for P2P and for Türkiye.
- Io "grow/eat" (Hole.io-like) and party obstacle races show the largest raw store numbers. Online, they are naturally many-player and server-backed. As 1v1 P2P or single-player-vs-bots, they fit only if adapted, for example as a timed same-seed "who eats more" duel.
- Single-player demand on portals is hypercasual and puzzle. The hub's "boring" complaint likely reflects low stakes and feedback rather than the genre itself (inference, no source on the hub's users).

### Gaps
- I found no published per-game play counts for Poki or CrazyGames, and no portal statistics for multiplayer share of playtime.
- The AppMagic Türkiye 2026 PDF was too large to fetch (over 10MB), so the full TR top-download list and its "2 3 4 Player" mention could not be verified directly.
- No numeric 2024–2026 data turned up for air hockey, archery, sumo, tank duels, trivia duels, or Tavla app downloads in TR.
- Sensor Tower blog pages are automated snapshots, so treat them with caution.

## Q2: Why each type is exciting, round length, touch controls, how the online 1v1 version works

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous sources on per-genre "excitement". The material below is mostly inference grounded in the cited sources. The common pattern is a one-gesture control (drag-to-aim or hold-to-charge), rounds under about 2 minutes, physics comedy or ragdoll chaos, and a visible score race with comebacks.

### Cited Findings
- Casual competitive games are described as short matches with no large time commitment, "intense bursts of gameplay", easy-to-pick-up mechanics and mobile-first design. — [Edgegap blog](https://edgegap.com/blog/the-unstoppable-rise-of-casual-competitive-games). The body is dated 2022 and the metadata 2026. It is a vendor blog.
- The same page links a claim that multiplayer players stay about twice as long as single-player players. This appears only in a related-article summary and is unverified. — [Edgegap blog](https://edgegap.com/blog/the-unstoppable-rise-of-casual-competitive-games)
- In casual puzzle games, about 75% of the US top-grossing 200 use competitive events, but only about 15% use head-to-head 1v1 events, which were "reasonably effective". The article is undated. — [GameRefinery](https://www.gamerefinery.com/event-spotlight-how-mobile-game-developers-can-boost-engagement-in-the-casual-puzzle-genre-with-competitive-1v1-events)
- Asynchronous PvP (as in Board Kings) "encourages rivalry and fun casual competitive play", and reuses single-player systems for PvP (Disney Emoji Blitz duels). The article is from about 2019–2021. — [GameRefinery](https://www.gamerefinery.com/4-great-examples-pvp-modes-casual-games/)
- Poki describes its party types as "mini-games, knockout rounds and last-player-standing" and board/classic games as "turn-based… planning and strategy". — [Poki multiplayer](https://poki.com/en/multiplayer)

### Inferences (per type; no direct sources for round lengths)
- **Turn-based physics shots (pool, penalty kick vs goalkeeper, basketball shootout, archery/sniper ragdoll, mini-golf):** drag-to-aim and release. Skill shows in angle and power. Big shots give bragging moments, and alternate turns create comeback tension. Rounds run about 1–5 minutes. Online play alternates turns, so latency barely matters.
- **Real-time physics duels (air hockey, sumo/bumper push, head-soccer-style 1v1, tank duel):** a joystick or finger-follow control. Physical comedy and instant rematches. Rounds run about 30–120 seconds. Simultaneous play needs host-authoritative state.
- **Same-seed parallel races (2-player reaction/tap race, obstacle runner, "who sorts or stacks faster", trivia duel):** each player plays an identical seeded level, and only progress and score are exchanged. Latency barely matters, and the format reuses the existing single-player games as PvP.
- **Turn-based board (Tavla/backgammon, Connect-4-style, dots and boxes, checkers):** strong TR cultural fit. Dice add comeback potential. Matches run 3–10 minutes.
- **Drawing and guessing:** high social comedy, but it needs 3+ players and chat or a word list in Turkish. As a 1v1 it is weak.
- **Io grow/eat:** satisfying growth fantasy, as Hole.io's 2025 record shows. As a 1v1, a 2-minute same-map duel with bots could work.

### Gaps
- No source quantified round lengths or "excitement" drivers by genre. Playtest data would be needed.

## Q3: Feasibility with HTML5 canvas and PeerJS (no game server)

### Takeaway
Turn-based shot games, board games and same-seed races are easy: one client simulates and sends the result or state. Real-time duels are medium if one peer acts as host and streams snapshots with interpolation of about 100–150ms. Deterministic lockstep with float physics is risky. Fast shooters are hard. Expect roughly 10–25% of P2P pairs to need a TURN relay, more on mobile CGNAT. PeerJS alone does not fix that.

### Cited Findings
- Deterministic lockstep sends only inputs, but "floating point determinism across platforms is hard". It suits 2–4 players, and everyone waits for the most-lagged player. — [Gaffer On Games: Deterministic Lockstep](https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/)
- Snapshot interpolation: at 10 snapshots/s the minimum interpolation delay is 100ms, and about 150ms is practical with jitter. Lost snapshots can be skipped. — [Gaffer On Games: Snapshot Interpolation](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation)
- State synchronization sends inputs plus state and does not rely on determinism. — [Gaffer On Games: Networked Physics](https://gafferongames.com/categories/networked-physics/)
- Lockstep was ruled out for non-deterministic physics engines (PhysX example). — [Meta developer blog, Fiedler](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/networked-physics-in-virtual-reality-networking-a-stack-of-cubes-with-unity-and-physx/)
- TURN relay requirement estimates:
  - About 10–25% of real-world connections (TURN vendor).
  - About 10–15% (EasyRTC docs).
  - About 20% reported by large vendors (discuss-webrtc).
  - Symmetric NAT and mobile carrier-grade NAT are the main causes.
  - Sources: [Metered blog](https://www.metered.ca/blog/what-is-a-turn-server/); [EasyRTC docs](https://github.com/gunjank/easyrtc/blob/master/docs/easyrtc_server_ice.md); [discuss-webrtc](https://groups.google.com/g/discuss-webrtc/c/5d_EJwM6iJM); [ExpressTURN on mobile networks](https://www.expressturn.com/blog/webrtc-calls-fail-on-mobile-networks)

### Inferences
- **Easy:**
  - Pool, penalty, basketball, archery and golf: the shooter simulates locally and sends either the final positions or the shot parameters plus resulting state, so no determinism is needed.
  - Tavla and other turn-based board games.
  - Same-seed races and trivia duels: send seed, progress and final score.
- **Medium:** air hockey, sumo/bumper and head-soccer 1v1. The host runs physics and sends snapshots at 15–30Hz, and the guest sends inputs and interpolates. The guest is disadvantaged by about 1 RTT. That is acceptable for casual play, especially with large slow objects.
- **Hard:** fast shooters and twitchy hitscan games (cheating, latency fairness, lag compensation), and anything with more than 2–4 players.
- **Cheating:** with no server, a malicious peer can fake results. That is acceptable for casual friend matches. Global leaderboards would need server validation.

### Gaps
- I found no PeerJS-specific production metrics, and no measurement of P2P connection success rates for Türkiye mobile carriers.

## Q4: Engagement features players expect in 2024–2026 casual multiplayer

### Takeaway
Sourced evidence supports friends, one-click invite and join, profiles, stats and streaks (CrazyGames' 2024 rollout), and competitive or 1v1 events plus async PvP in casual games (GameRefinery). Retention is falling industry-wide, which raises the value of meta features. I found no 2024–2026 source quantifying the value of rematch buttons, emotes, leagues or cosmetics specifically.

### Cited Findings
- CrazyGames' June 2024 social features: add friends, see online friends and what they play, one-click play together, custom profile names, game stats, play streaks. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/crazygames-hits-35m-users-for-browser-games-and-launches-social-multiplayer-features/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://pocketgamer.biz/crazygames-launches-new-social-features-for-instant-multiplayer-gaming)
- Poki multiplayer games connect via lobbies, private rooms or matchmaking. — [Poki multiplayer](https://poki.com/en/multiplayer)
- GameAnalytics: D1 retention declined in 2025 versus 2024, and top 25% of games have D1 just above 30% (top 10% around 40%). — [GameAnalytics 2026 benchmarks summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)
- Adjust: D1 retention across gaming apps was 27% in 2025. — [Adjust gaming app insights 2026](https://www.adjust.com/blog/gaming-app-insights-2026/)
- Hole.io's record 2025 came after adding level progression (Oct 2024), then raids, player profiles and a new city (Aug 2025). — [PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-most-downloaded-mobile-games-of-2025/)
- About 75% of top-grossing casual puzzle games use competitive events, and about 15% use 1v1 events. — [GameRefinery](https://www.gamerefinery.com/event-spotlight-how-mobile-game-developers-can-boost-engagement-in-the-casual-puzzle-genre-with-competitive-1v1-events)
- The 101 Okey Yalla app markets "Live & Voice", which shows that social voice is a selling point in the TR table-game segment. — [Sensor Tower TR Q2](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-android-top-5-free to play games-revenue-tr-620e5136afb48b4f71fc7c60)

### Inferences
- A minimum expected set for the hub, P2P-friendly:
  - invite link or room code (one-tap share to WhatsApp)
  - quick-match queue
  - instant rematch
  - preset emotes or quick chat (safer than free text)
  - best-of-3 series score
  - local win streak and stats
  - simple cosmetic unlocks (ball or skin colours)
  - daily challenge (same seed for everyone)
- Quick-match with strangers needs a lobby or matchmaking service. The PeerJS signaling server only brokers IDs, so a tiny presence list is needed.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–2026 quantitative studies on the impact of emotes, rematch, leagues or cosmetics in casual 1v1 games. The GameAnalytics PDF could not be searched for these features.
